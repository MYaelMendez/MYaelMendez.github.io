WHAT THE GATE ACTUALLY RETURNS

>_æ:// asimov://move stand
{
  "refused": true,
  "decision": "require_approval",
  "rules": ["ACT-001", "AUTH-002"],
  "allowed": false,
  "executed": false,
  "grants": false
}

Three fields carry the whole architecture. executed:false — nothing moved. grants:false — asking did not confer permission. rules — the refusal cites the clause that fired, so it can be argued with.

A refusal you cannot audit is just an error message. Ours names its rule.

Looking is free. Moving needs a signature. Claiming authority is denied outright — because an address is not a permission. Knowing asimov:// does not make you the principal.

WHAT THIS IS NOT

The renderer is not cutting edge. It is a z-buffer software rasteriser — 1990s technique, no PBR, no shadows. Our own WebGL path is 36× faster on the same card. The CUDA route exists for portability, not speed.

The chat is a grammar, not a model. Deliberate: it runs offline with no key. It is not intelligence and does not pretend to be.

The walking policy is Menlo's, not ours. We integrated it; we did not train it.

One machine. No second-box reproduction yet. That is the largest unverified claim in the stack, and it is stated rather than hidden.

Simulation only. No physical robot is attached to any surface here.

The honest claim is narrower and stronger than "advanced robotics":

Cutting-edge sovereignty architecture.
Deliberately primitive rendering.

The novel part is not that a laptop renders a robot. It is that the robot refuses you, cites the rule, and writes a receipt — and a stranger can reproduce that with two pip installs.
