THE ENVIRONMENT. THE ADDRESS SPACE. THE GATE.

Three threads converged on one idea: the estate's robots stop being a collection and become a fleet when they share an address space, a physics world, and a gate.

Nothing here is a plan. Everything below was executed and measured.

Two defects were found by measurement, not by looking:

1. Depth stagger only reads at one distance. Robots at y = 0 / −1.15 / −0.80: move closer and the forward body fills the frame while the other two leave it. They now stand on one plane, in a line.

2. A 9:16 frame holding three bodies of different sizes is mostly background, and the gate measures the frame. A ground plane was added — and testing it exposed a silent raster defect: the CuPy raster drops triangles past a screen bound (half=3.0 draws, half=6.0 disappears with no error).

Tests: agents/test_robot_env_film.py — 6/6, including the trace is byte-identical for a seed and differs for another.

2 · One address space

agents/robot_protocol.py · agents/robot_bridge.py · github-pages/robots.html

Six bodies. Every dialect keeps its own verbs; robot:// is the single front door and adds a fleet receipt on every route.

robot://asimov     14 verbs   udp · hybrid · livekit · sim     100%
robot://reachy     27 verbs   daemon · sim · mujoco           100%
robot://microduck  22 verbs   ble · udp · webrtc · sim         100%
robot://Soldier     7 verbs   mesh                             71%
robot://Xbot        7 verbs   mesh                             71%
robot://kira        7 verbs   mesh                             71%
The rule the index encodes. Whatever is built for one robot is provided for all — a missing verb is a named refusal, never a crash.

So the mesh bodies got the same seven routes. meshbot:// reports what is on disk (Soldier: 4 clips — Idle, Run, TPose, Walk · Xbot: 7 clips · kira: rigged, no clips) and refuses the rest by name:

plan animate walk   → allow    render plan, no hardware
plan animate nope   → refused  "Xbot has no clip 'nope'"
sim Walk            → refused  "no physics model — mesh only"
exec walk           → refused  "no actuators and no body — mesh only"

Coverage is 71%, not 100. sim and exec are genuinely absent, and the roster shows · for them. Declaring them "provided" would have been exactly the false claim the page warns about.

robot://fleet/ledger anchors every dialect ledger into one combined_sha256, so a fleet action is itself recorded.

3 · One gate

agents/publish_robot_env.py

The gate runs first. PASS → receipt + copy + manifest entry. FAIL → REFUSED receipt, nothing copied, exit 1.

The gate is never adjusted to fit the film. When a film fails, the scene is fixed and the film is re-made. That is why the ground plane exists and why the stagger was removed — the first cut failed, and the failure was real.

Honest limits

Film playback was not verified in the automation browser. It reports document.visibilityState === 'hidden', and Chrome suspends media loading for hidden documents — the <video> never issues a request. The bytes are reachable, and the film is verified by the gate, by ffprobe, and by an extracted frame. Playback in a visible tab is expected but unmeasured there.

media-manifest.json reports all_pass: false — from five older entries that carry no vision block. Pre-existing, not this film.

Xbot and Soldier ship animation clips and nothing plays them yet. meshbot:// plans them; rendering them is the next link.

Nothing is pushed. Every artifact above is local. Publishing is a separate, explicit step.

Why this is the beginning

The estate's robots were things on disk. They are becoming bodies in an address space: addressable by one scheme, standing in one world, recorded in one ledger, published through one gate.

That is the difference between a project and a platform. The next links are obvious once the substrate exists: play the clips, give the mesh bodies physics, drive the fleet from one console, and let the gate decide what ships.
