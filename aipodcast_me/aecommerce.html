Four Engineering Correctionsbefore standard

Essential distinctions for a trustworthy implementation. These strengthen the sovereignty argument.

🔌

WebMCP ≠ MCP

WebMCP exposes web capabilities to agents; a merchant-hosted remote MCP server is another pattern. Define an adapter contract rather than assuming every WebMCP endpoint accepts direct JSON-RPC calls.

🔐

AndroidMCP ≠ Trusted Wallet

Android Keystore, biometric authentication, payment credentials and native app handoffs each have different security properties. Biometrics gate a cryptographic operation; they do not prove a merchant received payment.

🚫

HTML <dialog> ≠ Authorization Boundary

An HTML dialog can be styled or imitated by page content. Consequential payments require a trusted native or payment-provider confirmation surface with a transaction-specific authorization challenge.

⚠️

Structured Commerce ≠ No Manipulation

Agents can still receive misleading prices, fake scarcity claims or malicious tool responses. Signed quotes, trustworthy merchant identities, total-price comparison, independent policy checks and auditable consent are still needed.

Procurement Lifecycle State Machineobservable · reversible

Every transition is observable, reversible where possible, and subject to its own authorization rules. Payment requires an idempotency key — an agent must never silently retry a purchase after an uncertain result.

Quote expired? → Re-quote and re-authorize. No silent retries on uncertain payment results.

Witness Receiptbuyer-owned · verifiable

A Witness receipt records the order reference, merchant identity, authorized amount, payment status, item and policy references, and verification evidence. It must not place full addresses, payment credentials or private purchase histories into a public repository.

The receipt becomes a reusable service reference.
The merchant becomes an interoperable capability provider. The buyer remains the principal. The receipt is locally exportable and SHA-256 verifiable.
First proof: a sandboxed jacket purchase with two mock merchants, live-style inventory and quote responses, a trusted simulated approval step, and a locally exportable Witness receipt. No real payments, accounts or addresses.
The buyer can bring their own agent, constraints, and transaction history to competing merchants.
The most important economic implication is not the disappearance of marketplaces. It is that the buyer is no longer required to adopt each merchant's interface.
