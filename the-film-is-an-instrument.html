The film is an instrument.

We used to make films about the robot. Then the film became a measuring device: a policy trace is rendered into pictures, the pictures are graded by a second instrument, and the two verdicts are compared. The picture can now contradict the physics — and the laboratory can observe itself, find a broken instrument, and instruct its own repair.

2026-11-22 · VICTUS / TEKNIUM RTX 3050 · LOCAL · NO CLOUD · AE-CONTINUITY

01The loop

A policy checkpoint walks. The trace is the physics. The film is the picture. Neither verdict is trusted alone.

policy trace→render · real CAD→supervise→ track→compare→receipt→next run

The last step is the recursion: the artefact of a run becomes the input of the next. The laboratory's own verdict is written to a hash chain, and its change field is read back as the next instruction.

03The verbs, done and refused

Nine verbs. Five the body performs; four it is governed to refuse. A verb that is refused is still a verb — it is declared, gated, and never executed.

The finding — corrected. The velocity command is body-frame: after a 0.8 rad/s turn a vy = 0.3 strafe moves the body along its new heading — 0.61 m in world −x. We first read that as a disobedience, because the per-segment verdict compared world axes against a body-frame command. Rotating the measured displacement into the body frame, every segment obeys, on both runnable policies. The policy was right; the instrument was wrong. The retraction is CORRECTION/01.
A correction, on the record. The first films did not carry the traversal. The renderer pinned the root (root[0,3] = root[1,3] = 0) and the camera followed the body, so the picture showed the gait and erased the path — 21 px of visual displacement against 3.118 m of physics. That was read as agreement; it was cancellation. A --free-root mode now holds the camera on the path: 21 px → 589 px, one track, 216 frames, 189 px/m. A film meant to teach must carry the motion, not merely show a body.

04The films

Each beat burns its source's hash into the frame; the full hash lives in the receipt. The citation is the hash of the delivered bytes, not of the source.

05The actor

The robot is a performer you script, not a model you re-render. A screenplay (actor/script/1) names the cast, and each cue names the actor, the verb (one of its own policy traces), the line, and the camera. The compiler renders each cue and cuts it into the film.

cast→cue · verb→trace → render→ line + camera→cut→receipt

The rule that makes it an actor rather than an animation: a bot's motion is its policy's. A verb with no trace is refused, never invented. And the cast is data — a second actor is a cast entry and a set of traces, not a code change. The mixture-of-robots surface now runs five of them under one authority.

06What is not true

  • We did not train the policy. Menlo did — Isaac Lab, PPO + AMP. We run it.
  • We did not write the renderer, the supervision engine, or Roboflow Supervision. We composed them and pointed them at each other.
  • No robot moved. Every number here is a simulation trace. asimov_exec stays dry-run; the Menlo SDK is not installed and there is no hardware.
  • The loop closes on process, not physics. The laboratory's change is a brief for the next run — not a retrained gait.
  • We did not model the robot. The CAD is Menlo's, and it is CERN-OHL-S-2.0 — an atlas rendered from it inherits those terms. Our generators are MIT; the rendered derivative is not.
  • One command on one checkpoint. The 65-scenario locomotion bench is unpulled; "it walks" is not yet "it walks under push".
