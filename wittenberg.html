What Project Wittenberg actually is

Not a metaphor first — a real project, described in its own words. From its About page:

# an ad hoc group of individuals dedicated to posting on the internet
# a cross-section of classic and historic texts written by Lutherans

"The project arose from the frustration felt by these librarians, archivists,
 scholars, pastors and others. A thorough search of the internet yielded few
 works by even Martin Luther..."

priority        documents by Luther, or about Luther
selection       "when a volunteer expresses an interest in working with them"
rights          public domain, or translators who yield free distribution
citation        "full scholarly citation is made" — in all cases
custody         Kroemer Library, Concordia Theological Seminary (CTSFW)
mirrors         CTSFW · Christian Classics Ethereal Library · Project Gutenberg
formats         HTML · ASCII/text · RTF
languages       English · German · French · Swedish
ambition        "the first step towards an international electronic library of Lutheranism"

Four structural moves matter, and none of them is "we put texts online":

1. It began from a failure to find. The frustration is the origin story — the corpus existed and was unreachable.

2. Citation is the trust mechanism. Every text carries its scholarly citation. Access is not the product; traceability is.

3. No single custody. Three mirrors, one corpus. The archive outlives any one host.

4. Volunteers, not a vendor. Ad hoc, informal, by interest.

Where it does not hold

Stated plainly, because a parallel that survives every test is a slogan.

Three real differences
  • Archive vs. runtime. Project Wittenberg digitises an existing, closed corpus. opensourceware is prospective — it builds new things, and the corpus grows by use. Not the same object.
  • Gratis vs. a rail. Project Wittenberg is purely volunteer, no revenue. This stack has a real economic rail — $133.65 paid, Doola affiliate, Stripe to come. That is a difference, not a detail.
  • Open code, gated execution. Project Wittenberg has no authority layer — you read a text or you do not. Here the code is open but execution is default-deny: publish, execute and merge require a scoped capability. That is a stricter posture than the original, not a looser one.

So the honest formulation: not a re-edition of Project Wittenberg, but the same gesture pointed at a different corpus — vernacular, cited, mirrored, no single custody — applied to a runtime instead of a library.
